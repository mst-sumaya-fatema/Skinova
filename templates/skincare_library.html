01 — Understanding Skin Types

Skin types are mainly related to how much oil your skin naturally produces. Your skin can also change with weather, age, hormones and other factors.

Oily Skin

Produces more sebum than needed. The skin may look shiny, especially around the forehead, nose and chin. Oily skin can also experience clogged pores and acne.

Dry Skin

Produces less natural oil. It may feel rough, tight or flaky and can sometimes appear dull. A suitable moisturizer helps support the skin barrier.

Combination Skin

Has different characteristics in different areas. The forehead, nose and chin may be oily, while the cheeks may feel normal or dry.

Normal Skin

Usually has a relatively balanced level of oil and moisture. It may not feel excessively oily or dry, although occasional concerns can still happen.

Sensitive Skin

May react easily to skincare products or environmental factors. Possible reactions include redness, itching, burning or irritation. Sensitivity can occur with any skin type.

Dehydrated Skin

A condition in which the skin lacks water. It may feel tight or look less fresh, even if it produces plenty of oil. It can affect every skin type.

Mature Skin

Skin naturally changes over time. It may become drier, less elastic or show more fine lines due to natural ageing and environmental exposure.

Acne-Prone Skin

This is a skin characteristic rather than a separate basic skin type. It describes skin that frequently develops pimples, blackheads or whiteheads.

Skin Barrier-Impaired Skin

When the protective skin barrier is not working properly, skin may become dry, tight, irritated or more sensitive to products and environmental factors.

Remember

Skin type and skin condition are not the same. For example, someone can have oily, sensitive and dehydrated skin at the same time. Skin needs can also change over time.

02 — Common Skin Concerns

Skin concerns can have different causes. Understanding them can help you make informed skincare decisions.

Acne & Pimples

Acne develops when hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells. Hormones, inflammation and other factors can also contribute.

Blackheads

Blackheads are open clogged pores. Their dark appearance is caused by the contents of the pore reacting with air, not simply by dirt.

Whiteheads

Whiteheads are closed clogged pores. They appear as small, pale or skin-coloured bumps.

Dark Spots & Acne Marks

These are areas of increased pigmentation that may remain after acne, irritation or inflammation. Sun exposure can make some dark spots more noticeable.

Melasma

Melasma causes patches of brown or grey-brown pigmentation, commonly on the face. Sun exposure and hormonal factors can contribute to it.

Uneven Skin Tone

Some areas of the skin may appear darker or lighter than surrounding areas. Sun exposure, inflammation and pigmentation changes can be contributing factors.

Excess Oiliness

Excess sebum can make the skin look shiny. It may also contribute to clogged pores in some people.

Dryness & Flakiness

Skin may feel rough, tight or flaky when it lacks sufficient moisture or natural oils.

Dehydration

Dehydrated skin lacks water. It may feel tight, uncomfortable or look less fresh, regardless of whether the skin is oily or dry.

Redness & Irritation

Redness, itching or burning may happen due to product reactions, environmental triggers or certain skin conditions.

Rough Skin Texture

Skin may feel uneven or rough due to dryness, clogged pores or other skin conditions.

Sun Damage

Repeated exposure to ultraviolet radiation can contribute to pigmentation, premature skin ageing and other long-term skin changes.

Under-Eye Darkness

Dark circles can be related to genetics, pigmentation, visible blood vessels, ageing or shadows caused by facial structure.

Fine Lines

Fine lines are small skin creases that can become more noticeable with natural ageing, sun exposure and repeated facial movements.

Enlarged-Looking Pores

Pores may appear more visible due to genetics, excess oil, clogged pores and changes in skin elasticity. Their appearance is normal.

Skin Itching

Itching can occur due to dryness, irritation, allergies or skin conditions. Persistent itching should be assessed by a qualified healthcare professional.

03 — Skincare Ingredients Guide

Skincare ingredients have different functions. Some support hydration, some help with clogged pores, and others support the skin barrier or protect against sun damage.

Hydrating & Moisturizing Ingredients

Hyaluronic Acid

A humectant that attracts and holds water in the skin. It is commonly used in hydrating serums and moisturizers.

Glycerin

A well-known humectant that attracts water and helps maintain skin hydration. It is found in many cleansers and moisturizers.

Panthenol (Vitamin B5)

A moisturizing ingredient that helps soothe the skin and support the skin barrier.

Squalane

An emollient that helps soften the skin and reduce moisture loss. It is used in many moisturizing products.

Urea

Helps attract water and soften rough skin. Its effect depends on the concentration used in a product.

Petrolatum

An occlusive ingredient that forms a protective layer to reduce water loss from the skin.

Skin Barrier & Soothing Ingredients

Ceramides

Lipids naturally found in the skin barrier. Skincare products containing ceramides can help support barrier function and reduce moisture loss.

Niacinamide (Vitamin B3)

Can support the skin barrier and help improve uneven skin tone and the appearance of excess oil. Skin tolerance varies.

Colloidal Oatmeal

A soothing ingredient that can help relieve dryness and support the skin barrier.

Allantoin

A skin-conditioning ingredient commonly used to soften and soothe the skin.

Centella Asiatica

A plant-derived ingredient used in skincare products designed to soothe and condition the skin.

Beta-Glucan

A moisturizing ingredient that can help soothe the skin and support hydration.

Ingredients for Acne & Clogged Pores

Salicylic Acid (BHA)

An exfoliating ingredient that can enter oil-filled pores and help reduce clogged pores. It may cause dryness or irritation in some people.

Benzoyl Peroxide

An acne treatment ingredient that helps reduce acne-causing bacteria and inflammation. It can irritate skin and bleach fabrics.

Azelaic Acid

Can help with acne and uneven pigmentation. It may cause mild irritation, especially on sensitive skin.

Sulfur

Used in some acne products to help reduce excess oil and support the management of certain blemishes. It can be drying.

Alpha Hydroxy Acids (AHAs)

Ingredients such as glycolic acid and lactic acid exfoliate the skin surface and can improve the appearance of uneven texture. They may increase sun sensitivity.

Retinoids

Vitamin A-related ingredients used for concerns such as acne and signs of skin ageing. They can cause irritation and require appropriate professional guidance.

Ingredients for Uneven Tone & Antioxidant Care

Vitamin C

An antioxidant that helps protect against environmental oxidative stress and may improve the appearance of uneven pigmentation.

Alpha Arbutin

An ingredient used in products designed to improve the appearance of uneven pigmentation and dark spots.

Tranexamic Acid

Used in some skincare products to help improve the appearance of uneven pigmentation. Results vary between individuals.

Licorice Root Extract

A plant-derived ingredient used in some products for soothing and improving the appearance of uneven skin tone.

Sun Protection Ingredients

Zinc Oxide

A mineral UV filter used in sunscreens to protect against UVA and UVB radiation.

Titanium Dioxide

A mineral UV filter used in sunscreens. The level of protection depends on the complete sunscreen formulation.

Broad-Spectrum UV Filters

Sunscreens use approved UV filters to help protect against ultraviolet radiation. Choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.

Important Ingredient Guide

An ingredient is not automatically suitable for everyone. Concentration, formulation, skin condition and frequency of use all matter. Introduce products carefully, avoid adding several strong active ingredients at once, and follow product directions. If you have a skin condition or persistent irritation, seek advice from a qualified dermatologist.

04 — Basic Skincare Tips

A good skincare routine does not need to be complicated. Consistency and gentle care are more important than using many products.

Gentle Cleansing

Use a gentle cleanser that removes dirt, sweat and excess oil without leaving your skin uncomfortably tight. Avoid harsh scrubbing.

Moisturizing

Use a suitable moisturizer to maintain hydration and support the skin barrier. Oily skin also needs appropriate moisturization.

Sun Protection

Use broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher sunscreen. Apply it as directed and reapply regularly, especially during prolonged outdoor exposure.

Patch Testing

Test new skincare products on a small area according to the product instructions before using them regularly. Stop using a product if a concerning reaction develops.

Avoid Over-Exfoliation

Using too many exfoliating products can irritate the skin barrier. Avoid combining multiple strong exfoliants without suitable guidance.

Do Not Pick Pimples

Picking or squeezing blemishes can increase irritation, inflammation and the chance of marks or scarring.

Remove Makeup Gently

Remove makeup before sleeping using a suitable makeup remover or cleanser. Avoid rubbing the skin harshly.

Keep Your Routine Simple

Start with basic cleansing, moisturizing and sun protection. Add other products only when needed and introduce them one at a time.

Check Product Expiry

Follow expiry dates and product storage instructions. Avoid using products that have changed in smell, colour or texture.

Keep Skincare Tools Clean

Clean makeup brushes and reusable skincare tools regularly. Avoid sharing personal items that touch the face.

05 — Healthy Lifestyle Habits

Skin health is influenced by more than skincare products. Everyday habits can support overall health and skin well-being.

Get Enough Sleep

Maintain a regular sleep schedule and aim for enough sleep for your age. Good sleep supports overall health and the body's natural recovery processes.

Manage Stress

Stress can affect overall well-being and may worsen some skin conditions in certain people. Try relaxing activities, hobbies or gentle exercise.

Stay Physically Active

Regular movement supports general health. Choose activities you enjoy and remember to cleanse away sweat gently when needed.

Stay Hydrated

Drink water regularly throughout the day. Your fluid needs can vary with weather, activity and individual health needs.

Keep Your Environment Clean

Keep frequently used personal items clean. Change pillowcases regularly and avoid letting dirty hands repeatedly touch your face.

Avoid Smoking and Vaping

Smoking and vaping can negatively affect overall health. Avoiding them is beneficial for your long-term well-being.

Protect Your Skin Outdoors

Use sunscreen, seek shade when possible and wear protective clothing during prolonged sun exposure.

Be Consistent

Healthy habits take time. Focus on regular, realistic routines instead of expecting immediate changes.

06 — Healthy Food Habits

A balanced diet supports overall health, including the normal functions of the skin. No single food can guarantee clear or perfect skin.

Eat a Variety of Fruits

Fruits provide vitamins, minerals, fibre and other nutrients. Include different seasonal fruits as part of a balanced diet.

Include Vegetables

Vegetables provide important nutrients and fibre. Try to include a variety of colourful vegetables in regular meals.

Choose Protein Sources

Foods such as fish, eggs, lentils, beans, chicken and nuts provide protein needed for normal body functions and tissue maintenance.

Include Healthy Fats

Foods such as fish, nuts, seeds and suitable plant oils provide fats that are part of a balanced diet.

Choose Whole Grains

Whole grains such as oats, brown rice and whole-wheat foods provide fibre and other nutrients.

Drink Enough Water

Drink water regularly and increase fluids when appropriate for heat or physical activity. Drinking excessive water does not automatically improve skin.

Notice Your Food Triggers

Some people notice that certain foods may worsen their acne, while others do not. Observe your own patterns without assuming that one food affects everyone.

Limit Excessively Sugary Foods

A diet high in foods that rapidly raise blood sugar may be associated with acne in some people. Balanced eating is more helpful than strict food rules.

Eat Regular, Balanced Meals

Include a variety of food groups and avoid unnecessary restrictive diets. Your body needs enough energy and nutrients to stay healthy.

A Note About Food & Acne

Research suggests that high-glycemic diets and some dairy products may be associated with acne in certain people, but the evidence is not the same for everyone. Food is only one possible factor. Do not remove entire food groups or follow restrictive diets just to change your skin. Seek advice from a qualified healthcare professional if you have concerns about your diet.

07 — Habits to Avoid

Some everyday habits can increase irritation or make existing skin concerns harder to manage.

Harsh Scrubbing

Scrubbing too hard can irritate the skin. Cleanse gently using your fingertips.

Using Too Many Products

Layering many new products at once makes it difficult to identify irritation and may overwhelm the skin.

Sleeping with Makeup

Leaving makeup on overnight may contribute to clogged pores or irritation for some people.

Using Products Not Meant for Skin

Avoid applying household chemicals or harsh non-skincare substances to your face.

Ignoring Irritation

Do not continue using a product that causes persistent burning, itching or irritation.

Skipping Sun Protection

Repeated unprotected sun exposure can contribute to sun damage and pigmentation.

A Gentle Reminder

Every skin is different. Healthy skin does not have to look perfect, poreless or identical to someone else's. Introduce new products carefully, keep your routine gentle and give products appropriate time to work. If you experience persistent, severe or painful skin problems, unusual changes, or a reaction that does not settle, consult a qualified dermatologist.
